# Niche robotics expansion and public professional contacts

Research reviewed: **7 October 2026**. This is a dated research snapshot, not a guarantee of current deployment counts, profile access, or email delivery. No outreach was performed. Only the assigned research expansion, contacts, updates, and notes files were changed.

## Selection and evidence standard

The five additions extend the existing eight-company niche map into underwater survey operations, robotic charging infrastructure, forestry teleoperation, physical inventory intelligence, and grocery micro-fulfillment. Each addresses a specific operational bottleneck, has a substantive technical architecture to investigate, and has evidence beyond a fundraising announcement or isolated video. They were selected for complementary technical and economic questions, rather than to maximize company count.

`expansion-niche.json` contains five companies and 28 source links. All 28 sources were opened during research and reopened in the final URL audit. Sources include company engineering pages, named customer accounts, an industry award body, a research institution, a granted patent, founder/operator interviews, credible reporting, and two exact Reddit discussions. Source `type` is constrained by the atlas schema; customer accounts, institutional profiles, investor accounts, founder essays, and the patent consequently use `report`, with their actual provenance stated in `note`. A patent is not labeled as a peer-reviewed paper.

`stage: Deployed` describes established use of a company's product, not every capability or product variant. Rocsys M1 remains a pilot, Kodama's many-to-one autonomous operation remains unproven, and published expansion plans are not counted as deployments. Metrics are dated and attributed. Vendor/investor claims are not silently upgraded to independent measurements. No proprietary commercial stack is implied to be available on GitHub.

`updates-niche.json` only supplies concise summary, focus, and edge fields for the eight existing entries. It does not rewrite their underlying evidence.

## What was read and what remains uncertain

### Bedrock Ocean Exploration

Read the primary navigation/processing architecture page, the two-AUV field-trial account, the August 2026 First Marine Solutions partnership, the April 2026 leadership/operations update, and TechCrunch's June 2025 reporting. The trial provides a concrete example of neighboring survey sections and overlapping bathymetry/side-scan products. INS/Doppler navigation, onboard data quality checks, Trident fleet operation, and Mosaic data delivery make the complete survey workflow the relevant technology thesis.

Dense sample grids and relative overlap agreement do not establish absolute georeferencing accuracy. The TechCrunch positioning figure is explicitly a 2025 observation, while current quality claims remain company-reported. The Aberdeen operations hub is a named announced partnership and training arrangement, not an independently established throughput figure. A public worldwide ocean map remains an ambition.

Matthew Tirman became CEO in April 2026; he is not a founder. Founder Charles Chiau is currently described as CTO. Anthony DiMare's chairman transition is supported by a 2024 founder announcement, with its historical date retained. A publicly reachable fleet-specification PDF carried a **CONFIDENTIAL** footer and was excluded from the entry and contact research; its availability was not treated as permission to republish its details.

### Rocsys

Read the S2 engineering page, the April 2026 ITS/Port of Long Beach deployment, the M1 multibay announcement, current company leadership/deployment material, UNIIQ's founding technical account, and the exact r/SelfDrivingCars discussion. Seven-axis motion, camera-based inlet pose localization, mechanical compliance, connector locking, protected sensors, and industrial controls are specific to the connector-handling bottleneck.

The named port equipment is a **human-operated** electric Taylor container handler. It is evidence for robotic charging, not autonomous driving. The M1 announcement describes pilot-stage infrastructure and targets large-scale deployment in 2027. Company pages disagree about S2 launch timing, so the entry does not assert a definitive launch year. Cleaning and inspection are explicitly still in development.

The company-reported deployment map and charging-success, labor-saving, and utilization claims lack an independent fleet audit. The Reddit discussion is useful for questions about amortization, shared cleaning/inspection labor, depot utilization, and alternative charging interfaces; anonymous comments do not verify a cost model. The Joost van de/der Weijde spelling varies across official and historical pages; the contact is matched to the same public professional profile rather than to a guessed email.

### Kodama Systems

Read current RS1 architecture, the December 2024 field-operation article, the August 2025 Weiler S340 demonstration, the remote-operator Kaleb Shird interview, the March 2026 Forest Invest episode description/chapter guide, and available WSJ reporting about Weyerhaeuser. The podcast audio was **not** listened to or transcribed; the source note says what was reviewed.

The technical account distinguishes satellite teleoperation, local LiDAR/cameras/computing, machine controls, and a separate emergency-stop/hold-to-run circuit. The vendor's description is not an independent safety certification. The 98% remote-operation figure in early material is a goal, not an achieved rate. Local crews still service, fuel, grease, and recover equipment.

WSJ's article is subscription restricted. Its indexed report and customer quotations support a Weyerhaeuser **pilot** with remote operation and AI-assisted navigation; full text was not represented as freely accessible. Commercial teleoperation is better established than many-to-one autonomy. Site-dependent demonstration throughput is not a controlled productivity comparison. Forest carbon-storage ambitions are separate from demonstrated logging economics.

### Verity

Read IKEA's customer account, IFR's June 2026 IERA award account, Maersk's implementation description, ETH's technical-commercialization profile, Raffaello D'Andrea's December 2024 founder essay, and the exact r/technology discussion. The navigation, barcode capture, autonomous charging, fleet coordination, and warehouse-system reconciliation workflow is supported across several sources.

IKEA's 250-plus drones at 73 locations in nine countries is a **2024 customer snapshot**. IFR's approximately 200 warehouses is a **2026 company-wide figure**. Different dates and denominators are preserved rather than combined. Maersk's initial estimated savings are not universal achieved ROI. The founder's reliability claims are self-reported and were not adopted as independent performance metrics.

The exact Reddit thread includes a self-identified former shipping/receiving worker's perspective on inventory counting, alongside noise/job concerns. Identity and statements are unverified sentiment. The commercial stack is proprietary. Verity's homepage resolves but produced a minimal JavaScript-rendered response in the research reader; this is an access/rendering limitation, not evidence of a broken website. A technical whitepaper host did not yield readable content and was not included as a source.

### Fulfil Solutions

Read the company engineering page, Amazon's May 2026 customer update, granted patent US10543942B2 including its abstract and relevant description/figures, DCVC's deployment account, and current company leadership. Purpose-built trays, tower storage, fixed dispense stations, mobile ShopBots, packing order, and refrigerated enclosures make this an interesting co-design of goods handling and storage rather than a general arm demo.

Patent embodiments describe possible mechanisms; they do not prove that every mechanism is in the deployed product. Patent inventorship also does not establish founder identity. Amazon confirms the Plymouth Meeting installation, a 10,000-square-foot facility, approximately 12,000 items, and **employees finalizing orders at handoff**. This does not prove labor-free replenishment, maintenance, exception handling, or delivery. Planned additional facilities are not counted as operating sites.

DCVC is an investor. Its accuracy and packing-time figures have no public test denominator and were not elevated to verified metrics. Mir Aamir is the current CEO. Gilad Almogy's current founder/CEO title on Fulfil's board page refers to **Ultima Genomics**; a separate GEN founder biography supports his historical Fulfil founding role. The contact roster is deliberately limited to him because other names found in patents, employee listings, or databases lacked adequate founder provenance. The unrelated `fulfil.io` ERP company was excluded.

A professional channel is not an invitation to contact, proof of message delivery, or a confirmed current employer. Company routes are marked `kind: company` and described as company intake, rather than direct founder inboxes. No email patterns were guessed, and no brokers, private data, or outreach tools were used.

For the five additions, official company roles, institutional material, founder essays, historical announcements, and exact indexed professional profiles were used together. Founder identity does not follow automatically from a board seat, investor status, patent inventor listing, or current CEO title. Where no direct professional channel was verified, the record says so rather than synthesizing an address.

Two published direct professional email routes were included:

1. **Remo Diethelm:** `rdiethelm@anybotics.com` appears in the official ANYbotics Kindr README/documentation as a project-maintainer contact. This is a published legacy engineering route, not assurance of present employment or mailbox delivery.

Many exact LinkedIn profiles were established by an official outbound link or a matched public indexed biography, while direct reads returned HTTP 999, login limits, or robots restrictions. `verification` describes identity/provenance; each affected channel's `note` states the direct-access restriction. A restricted profile is not a broken link, and no content behind its access boundary was claimed to have been read. Founder essays and company posts that were readable were reviewed separately from profile pages.

## Social discovery and link-access audit

X/Twitter and Reddit were searched for all five additions. Exact substantive Reddit discussions were found and read for Rocsys and Verity. Several X searches yielded sparse indexing, unrelated name collisions, or unconfirmable reposts; **no exact strong X thread was verified for these five**, and no invented thread URLs, search-result URLs, or unrelated GitHub repositories were inserted to satisfy a quota. Kodama's first-person operator interview supplied better operational evidence than thin anonymous mentions. This is a documented coverage limitation.

The final audit reopened all **28 entry source URLs**, with each returning a recognizable source page. No entry source returned an identified 404. This check establishes that links resolve through the research reader, not that a logged-out user will always receive full text or identical regional rendering.

Access limitations are distinguished from broken links:

- WSJ: working article route, subscription-restricted full text; available indexed reporting reviewed.
- LinkedIn founder profiles: exact route and identity corroboration, frequent HTTP 999/login restrictions; readable essay/post pages reviewed where available.
- Maersk: visible implementation content, with an additional account-only section.
- Verity homepage: resolves, minimal JavaScript response in the reader.
- Excluded whitepaper/PDF or canonical-report routes: insufficient readable access or inappropriate publication status; not inserted as verified technical references.

JSON syntax, required contact fields, channel kind/verification values, review dates, source URL format, minimum four sources per addition, and concise summary/edge limits were checked successfully. These checks do not certify undisclosed unit economics, delivery to a mailbox, proprietary safety performance, or independence of company-reported operational data.
